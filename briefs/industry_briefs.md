# Industry briefs: Kenya Opportunity Radar

*One short brief per industry group, generated from real KNBS and CBK data. Balanced weights unless stated. Sources: KNBS Quarterly GDP Q1 2026 (Table 2); CBK Bank Supervision Annual Report 2025 (Table 18); CBK Credit Officer Survey, June 2026; KNBS Economic Survey 2026 (Annex 1).*

## 1. Financial Services (Lead; score 77/100; stable across weights)

- Size: 10.8% of GDP. Growth: 6.5% in 2025, 6.3% in Q1 2026, 9.0% a year over 2021 to 2025.
- Bank credit: NPL ratio 3.3%, net credit demand 22.0 points (banks reporting higher demand minus lower).
- Rank range across five weighting scenarios: 1 to 1.

**Evidence.** Lending is growing (total domestic credit +10.1% in 2025) and the sector has the cleanest book among bank sectors. Mobile money transfers fell 5.3% in 2025, a sign that payments are changing shape.

**Conversation starters.**
- How are you growing lending while keeping bad loans low?
- Where do you see digital payments moving, given mobile money value fell in 2025?

**How to use it.** Lead with risk, regulation and digital operating-model topics.

## 2. Consumer & Retail (Lead; score 63/100; stable across weights)

- Size: 10.6% of GDP. Growth: 5.5% in 2025, 5.8% in Q1 2026, 6.3% a year over 2021 to 2025.
- Bank credit: NPL ratio 20.2%, net credit demand 53.2 points (banks reporting higher demand minus lower).
- Rank range across five weighting scenarios: 2 to 4.

**Evidence.** Trade shows the strongest credit demand of any business sector (65% of banks report higher demand), yet 19.9% of trade loans are non-performing. Tourism and hotels lend at a 22.9% NPL ratio, and more banks see demand falling than rising (25% vs 19%).

**Conversation starters.**
- Working capital is in demand: how are you managing supplier and stock financing?
- Tourist arrivals grew 6.2% to 2,550.6 thousand in 2025; how are you capturing that demand?

**How to use it.** Pair growth content with credit-risk and cash-management insight.

## 3. Public Sector & Development (Lead; score 61/100; stable across weights)

- Size: 12.2% of GDP. Growth: 5.9% in 2025, 5.9% in Q1 2026, 5.5% a year over 2021 to 2025.
- Bank credit: NPL ratio n/a%, net credit demand n/a points (banks reporting higher demand minus lower).
- Rank range across five weighting scenarios: 2 to 3.

**Evidence.** Public administration and education are 12.2% of the economy. Ordinary revenue and grants grew 15.6% in 2025 against expenditure growth of 4.7%. External debt service was 4.9% of GDP, or 31.1% of export earnings.

**Conversation starters.**
- How is your organisation planning around tighter fiscal space and higher debt service?
- Where could better public financial management and reporting free up capacity?

**How to use it.** No bank-credit signal exists, so the rank rests on size and growth only.

## 4. Energy & Resources (Grow; score 45/100; stable across weights)

- Size: 3.7% of GDP. Growth: 7.9% in 2025, 5.7% in Q1 2026, 3.5% a year over 2021 to 2025.
- Bank credit: NPL ratio 9.6%, net credit demand 26.0 points (banks reporting higher demand minus lower).
- Rank range across five weighting scenarios: 4 to 7.

**Evidence.** Electricity consumption rose 9.6% in 2025 to 11,785.5 GWh. Energy and water has one of the cleanest loan books (6.8% NPL). Mining grew 9.1% in Q1 2026 from a small base.

**Conversation starters.**
- Demand for power is up almost 10%: what does that mean for your capacity and grid plans?
- How are you funding the next investment cycle?

**How to use it.** Strong growth from a small base; good for focused, high-value conversations.

## 5. Agribusiness (Grow; score 44/100; sensitive across weights)

- Size: 18.3% of GDP. Growth: 3.1% in 2025, 4.9% in Q1 2026, 3.1% a year over 2021 to 2025.
- Bank credit: NPL ratio 19.3%, net credit demand 19.0 points (banks reporting higher demand minus lower).
- Rank range across five weighting scenarios: 2 to 7.

**Evidence.** Agriculture is the largest sector (18.3% of GDP) but grew only 3.1% in 2025 and 4.9% in Q1 2026. Tea marketed production fell 8.0% while horticultural exports grew 13.8% and milk sold centrally 11.5%. Bank NPLs are 19.3%.

**Conversation starters.**
- Horticulture exports are growing while tea fell: how is your portfolio shifting?
- How are you protecting margins against weather and price swings?

**How to use it.** Rank is sensitive to weights: high on scale, low on growth.

## 6. Real Estate & Construction (Grow; score 42/100; stable across weights)

- Size: 17.1% of GDP. Growth: 4.9% in 2025, 5.5% in Q1 2026, 4.5% a year over 2021 to 2025.
- Bank credit: NPL ratio 24.1%, net credit demand 9.2 points (banks reporting higher demand minus lower).
- Rank range across five weighting scenarios: 5 to 7.

**Evidence.** Cement consumption jumped 20.3% and construction output rose 9.4% in 2025, but this group carries the weakest loan quality: 24.1% overall, 29.5% for building and construction alone. Credit officers see little change in demand.

**Conversation starters.**
- Building activity is picking up: how are you managing payment delays and project finance?
- Which of your projects are most exposed to the high level of stressed loans in the sector?

**How to use it.** Activity is up while credit risk is the highest; position around risk and project delivery.

## 7. Technology, Media & Telecom (Grow; score 34/100; sensitive across weights)

- Size: 3.7% of GDP. Growth: 4.8% in 2025, 5.0% in Q1 2026, 7.8% a year over 2021 to 2025.
- Bank credit: NPL ratio 16.6%, net credit demand 8.0 points (banks reporting higher demand minus lower).
- Rank range across five weighting scenarios: 5 to 9.

**Evidence.** Information and communication grew 7.8% a year over 2021 to 2025, the second-fastest track record. Mobile subscriptions reached 78.4 million (+9.8%) but mobile money transfers fell 5.3%. Bank data covers transport and communication together (16.6% NPL), so credit signals are shared.

**Conversation starters.**
- Subscriptions keep growing but mobile money value fell: where is the next revenue pool?
- How are you thinking about cybersecurity and data protection as services scale?

**How to use it.** Strong long-run growth; credit read-across is imperfect.

## 8. Health (Selective; score 32/100; sensitive across weights)

- Size: 2.5% of GDP. Growth: 5.5% in 2025, 4.3% in Q1 2026, 4.8% a year over 2021 to 2025.
- Bank credit: NPL ratio n/a%, net credit demand n/a points (banks reporting higher demand minus lower).
- Rank range across five weighting scenarios: 6 to 10.

**Evidence.** Health is 2.5% of GDP and grew 5.5% in 2025, with 4.3% growth in Q1 2026. Licensed doctors and dentists grew 4.9% to 18,561. CBK reports no health bank sector, so the credit pillars are neutral.

**Conversation starters.**
- How are you funding expansion in capacity and equipment?
- What would make universal coverage workable for your organisation?

**How to use it.** Small and under-evidenced here; a selective, relationship-led play.

## 9. Manufacturing (Selective; score 30/100; stable across weights)

- Size: 8.5% of GDP. Growth: 2.0% in 2025, 4.4% in Q1 2026, 2.4% a year over 2021 to 2025.
- Bank credit: NPL ratio 18.4%, net credit demand 30.0 points (banks reporting higher demand minus lower).
- Rank range across five weighting scenarios: 6 to 9.

**Evidence.** Growth is the weakest of the ten groups (2.0% in 2025, 2.4% a year over 2021 to 2025). Output rose 3.5% to KSh 3.8 trillion. Credit demand is solid (net +30) but 18.4% of loans are non-performing.

**Conversation starters.**
- Demand for credit is rising: where are you investing, and what is holding back volumes?
- How are input costs and competitiveness affecting your margins?

**How to use it.** Large and credit-hungry but slow-growing; select accounts, do not blanket-target.

## 10. Transport & Logistics (Selective; score 23/100; stable across weights)

- Size: 10.3% of GDP. Growth: 3.7% in 2025, 3.6% in Q1 2026, 4.8% a year over 2021 to 2025.
- Bank credit: NPL ratio 16.6%, net credit demand 8.0 points (banks reporting higher demand minus lower).
- Rank range across five weighting scenarios: 8 to 10.

**Evidence.** Rail freight grew 11.3% to 8.4 million tonnes while air passengers fell 1.2%. Growth in Q1 2026 was 3.6%, the lowest of the ten groups.

**Conversation starters.**
- Rail freight is up 11%: what does that mean for your logistics costs?
- How are you adjusting to flat air traffic?

**How to use it.** Lowest score; keep to selective, account-driven outreach.
